What are points?

Points are the score Hacker News shows next to a story. People vote stories up, and HN ranks a story by dividing its points by a power of the time since it was submitted (HN FAQ).

HN's API docs define score as a story's score (or a poll option's count), and their comment example has no score field. So comments carry no points in this data, and every points number here is story points.

Each score was recorded when the item was fetched, so the newest stories may still be gaining points.

    How to read these cards

    The cards from h300 onward copy their thresholds from the older cards, so they are exploratory, not blind pre-registrations. The odd-and-fun word lists and thresholds were set while looking at earlier summaries. Theme cards (H319 onward) compare the latest 12 months with every earlier story since 2006, matched on title only. A falling share is not proof that adoption fell. Four older cards (H112, H113, H114, H119) stay frozen because they need comments or source domains, which the archive does not hold. Scores are a snapshot of a short window. Cards H135-H149 use a fixed snapshot window and do not refresh daily. Wrong guesses stay on the page.

      Submit a hypothesis

      Guess something about Hacker News, say in advance what the data must show, and the site checks it.

      1. Fork the repo.
      2. Copy a card in hypotheses/ to hypotheses/hNNN-your-name.json, using the next free number.
      3. Fill it in. Write the verdict thresholds before you look at the result, and include a rule that refutes you.
      4. Run node tests/hypotheses.test.js to see your verdict.
      5. Open a pull request with only that file.

      A check runs on the PR. It fails if the card is malformed, cannot fail, points at missing data, or touches other files. The owner reviews and merges, then the card is published on the next deploy. Failed hypotheses are shown too.

      A fall from a peak is not a return to the past

      The same test on other topics

      Same chosen period for every row, the latest 12 months. Only the baseline changes. Share of story titles that match the topic's word list.

      How to read trends

      1. Fix the latest window. Pick one recent period, here the last 12 complete months, and do not change it.
      2. Swap the baseline. Compare it to the topic's peak and to all earlier history. A trend that looks different against each is the interesting kind.
      3. Name the pattern. Reversal: below the peak, above history. Peak decay: below both. Emergence: above both. Composition shift: the mix inside the topic changes, like data centers moving toward power, water and local opposition. A pattern matters when it supports an argument you can test, not only when the windows disagree.
      4. Watch vocabulary drift. People rename things. "Basic income" and "UBI" are one idea with two words, and a single word hides that.
      5. Check the dictionary. List what the word list matches and misses. "Ask YC:" is the forum, not the company. "UBI" is also a Linux file system. Read a sample of matches before you trust a number.

      Method

      Every number on this site is computed from public Hacker News data, in your browser.

      • Data. Every Hacker News story since 2006, from a public archive: titles, links, points and comment counts, plus daily counts of stories, comments, jobs and polls. Comment text and author names are held only for the newest items, which a daily job pulls from the public HN API. Exact counts and dates are in data/archive/manifest.json and data/manifest.json.
      • Centers of gravity. For each lens (author, domain, words, hour, weekday, month, points, comments, type) we measure how concentrated points are, for example what share the top 1% of stories hold.
      • Year by year. Themes are the share of a year's story titles that name the theme. Top sites are counted from each month's top 20 domains. Both are built from the full archive.
      • Questions. Each card asks one question, sets the number that would answer it before looking, and shows the result with its caveats. The answer is computed from the data, never typed. Wrong guesses stay on the page.
      • Today's story. Each day a script reads the monthly title counts and looks for topics whose answer changes with the window you pick, or whose latest month jumps above the year before. It ranks them, skips any topic shown in the last 90 days, writes the word list it used and publishes the top one by itself. Every story states its dates and needs at least 30 matching stories in the smaller window. It tests the topic on the current data and is not a prediction.
      • Download. Every saved week, as CSV or JSON. What each column means.
      • Daily checks. Each daily update is checked before it is saved: row counts and file hashes against the manifest, no repeated ids, no unknown item types, and a few wide ranges, such as the share of stories that are dead or deleted. A second check compares the new data with the last saved day: the window keeps its size, the scan never moves back and the archive only grows. If a check breaks, that day is not saved, the site stays on the last good data and one issue is opened. The checks and their limits.
      • Caveats. About 89% of items are comments, which have no points, so point and comment lenses use stories only. The archive holds low points for 2023 and 2026, so charts that use points flag or skip those years. Times are UTC.
