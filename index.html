What are points?

Points are the score Hacker News shows next to a story. People vote stories up, and HN ranks a story by dividing its points by a power of the time since it was submitted (HN FAQ).

HN's API docs define score as a story's score (or a poll option's votes), and their comment example has no score field. So comments carry no points in this data, and every points number here is story points.

Each score was recorded when the item was fetched, so the newest stories may still be gaining points.

      Submit a hypothesis

      Guess something about Hacker News, say in advance what the data must show, and the site checks it.

      1. Fork the repo.
      2. Copy a card in hypotheses/ to hypotheses/hNNN-your-name.json. Use the next free number.
      3. Fill it in. Write the verdict thresholds before you look at the result, and include a rule that refutes you.
      4. Run node tests/hypotheses.test.js to see your verdict.
      5. Open a pull request with only that file.

      A check runs on the PR. It fails if the card is malformed, cannot fail, points at missing data, or touches other files. The owner reviews and merges, then the card is published on the next deploy. Failed hypotheses are shown too.

      How it works

      Everything here is computed in your browser from public Hacker News data.

      • Data. The latest 1,000,000 HN items (stories, comments, jobs, polls), pulled from the public HN API. A daily job adds new items and keeps the latest million.
      • Centers of gravity. For each lens (author, domain, words, hour, weekday, month, score, comments, type) we measure how concentrated points are, for example what share the top 1% of stories hold.
      • Hypotheses. Each card states a guess and the number that would prove or refute it before looking. The verdict is computed from the data, never typed.
      • Caveats. About 89% of items are comments, which show no points, so point and comment lenses use stories only. The newest stories may not have final scores. Times are UTC.
